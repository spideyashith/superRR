# Pressure definition: Leverage Index (LI)

Stage 3. Every number below is generated by `py -m src.pressure`. The full
ball-level table is `outputs/tables/pressure_ball.parquet`.

## 1. In plain English

Pressure is how much the next ball can change the result. In a close chase with wickets falling and few balls left, one ball can swing the win probability by 20-40 points. In the first over of a one-sided game it barely moves. Leverage Index measures exactly that swing, before the ball is bowled, and gives every delivery a weight.

LI is a property of the match state, not of the player. A batter or bowler does not make a ball high-pressure by reputation; the score, wickets, balls remaining and target do.

## 2. Definition

For a pre-ball state `s` in an innings:

```
LI(s) = sum over outcomes o of  P(o | s) * |WP_after(s, o) - WP_before(s)|
```

- `WP_before(s)` is the out-of-fold win probability of the batting side before the ball.
- `WP_after(s, o)` is the win probability of the same side after outcome `o`, from the same fold model.
- Outcomes: 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 runs, a wicket, and extras (1 run, no legal ball). The eight probabilities come from the empirical distribution in the training folds, in the same innings, phase, wicket bucket and situation bucket; cells with fewer than 100 training balls fall back to broader bins.
- A wicket is simulated as 0 runs plus one wicket on a legal ball; extras as one run and no legal ball. Rare 5-run deliveries are folded into 4.

QA: the fold model reproduces the saved OOF `WP_before` with maximum absolute difference **0.00e+00**. LI never crosses the innings break: each innings uses its own model and its own state sequence, so no WPA or LI is computed between the last ball of innings 1 and the first ball of innings 2. WPA is always `p_post - p_pre`, including the terminal ball: `p_post` is set to 0 or 1, so a win from 0.70 has WPA 0.30, not 1.0.

The eight next-ball probabilities are conditional on the state bins, mutually exclusive outcomes, and normalised to sum to 1.0. The test suite checks the sum on every one of the 183,167 deliveries, not a sample.

## 3. Normalisation and recommended threshold

The innings-1 win-probability model is materially weaker than the innings-2 model (OOF AUC 0.690 vs 0.897). A single pooled LI scale would therefore let the sharper chase model dominate the ranking of balls. The main version normalises LI **within each innings** so the league mean is 1.0 per innings; the pooled version is reported for comparison.

Within-innings quartile cut-offs: innings 1 = **1.131**, innings 2 = **1.370**. Tercile cut-offs: 1.057 / 1.107. Median: 0.936 / 0.700.

**Recommended: top quartile of within-innings LI** (HIGH), with the bottom half as ORDINARY. It is the PRD's main definition, it guarantees that innings-1 pressure is represented rather than drowned by the stronger innings-2 model, and it leaves a clean middle band out of the binary comparison. Top tercile, LI > 1.5 and LI > 2 are sensitivity variants.

| innings | within_innings_quartile | pooled_quartile |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.250 | 0.068 |
| 2 | 0.250 | 0.448 |

The table above shows why pooling is not the main version: one innings gets materially more than 25% of the high-pressure balls under a pooled cut-off.

Raw LI is the expected absolute WP change and lies in [0, 1]; the maximum is 0.104 in innings 1 and 0.546 in innings 2. Dividing by the innings mean puts the tail on a league-average scale: the 99th percentile is 2.63 in innings 1 and 5.39 in innings 2, and a final ball can exceed 30. For Stage 4's continuous weights I will winsorise normalised LI at the innings 99th percentile, so one delivery cannot dominate a player's rating; the binary HIGH/ORDINARY split is unaffected.

## 5. SPS cross-check

The Situational Pressure Score is a transparent 0-1 composite of required-rate gap (or par-rate gap in innings 1), wickets in hand, balls remaining, closeness (|WP - 0.5|), phase, recent collapse, dot-ball streak and knockout status. Weights: closeness 0.40, rrr_gap 0.15, wickets 0.10, collapse 0.10, knockout 0.10, balls 0.05, phase 0.05, dot_streak 0.05. SPS is a sanity check, not the rating input.

| innings | n | spearman | p_value | pearson |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 95425 | 0.2008 | 0.0000 | 0.2070 |
| 2 | 87742 | 0.4979 | 0.0000 | 0.4278 |
| pooled | 183167 | 0.4214 | 0.0000 | 0.3230 |

Both correlations are positive, which is the sanity check we wanted, but they are far from one: LI is non-linear in win probability and spikes when one wicket can flip the result, while SPS is a smooth additive score anchored in late-game jeopardy. The gap is largest in innings 1, where LI is highest while the game is still close and SPS is still low because few balls have been used. That is why LI, not SPS, drives the ratings, and why SPS is reported as a cross-check rather than a substitute.

### What the data says

The PRD's prior was that strike rate falls, dot% rises and dismissal rate rises under pressure. The table is the honest test, not a confirmation. Raw strike rate and dot% move in both directions; the state-adjusted columns are the ones to trust.

Death overs are the clearest exception: high-pressure death balls have a strike-rate difference of **+13.81** runs per 100 balls and a state-adjusted runs difference of **+1.86** per 100. Late-overs batting is not defensive: batters trade wickets for boundaries because the required rate leaves no alternative. That is a real tactical response, not a data error, and it is why the rating must use continuous LI weights rather than reward only low strike rates.

Where the adjusted wicket delta is positive, high-leverage balls also produce more wickets than the state predicts; where it is negative, the opposite. The middle-order and powerplay cells are the ones to read most cautiously because they contain the fewest high-pressure balls.

## 7. Worked examples

These hand-built states are scored by the full-data model. Main LI uses the fold models; the counterfactual table is for interpretation only. `li_raw` is the expected absolute WP change; `li_innings` puts it on the league-average scale used by the thresholds.

| example | innings | runs_required | balls_remaining | wickets_in_hand | batting_wp | li_raw | li_innings | side_pressure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| First innings: 25/3 after 4 overs | 1 | — | 102 | 7 | 0.274 | 0.008 | 0.719 | batter_pressure |
| 1 run from 3 balls | 2 | 1.000 | 3 | 5 | 0.993 | 0.006 | 0.299 | bowler_pressure |
| 18 runs from 6 balls | 2 | 18.000 | 6 | 5 | 0.526 | 0.104 | 5.121 | bowler_pressure |
| 30 runs from 30 balls, 8 wickets in hand | 2 | 30.000 | 30 | 8 | 0.993 | 0.002 | 0.074 | bowler_pressure |
| 60 runs from 30 balls, 3 wickets in hand | 2 | 60.000 | 30 | 3 | 0.060 | 0.013 | 0.652 | batter_pressure |

The largest raw LI is **0.104** for *18 runs from 6 balls* (batting WP 0.53, label **bowler_pressure**). With 1 run from 3 balls the batting side's WP is 0.99, so the model labels it **bowler_pressure** and gives raw LI 0.006. That is not a contradiction: the batting side is already a strong favourite, so the absolute win-probability change is smaller than in a 50-50 chase even though a wicket would be dramatic. LI measures absolute leverage, not drama.

The first-innings state is the one that needs a scale warning: raw LI is 0.008, but innings-1 raw LI averages about 0.011 across the league, so its normalised value is 0.72. Raw values must not be compared across innings; the normalised version is the comparable one.

Two outputs are deliberately left as the model produced them because they are surprising. First, 30 off 30 with 8 wickets in hand gets a batting WP of 0.992 and almost no leverage (raw LI 0.002): the model is overconfident in a comfortable chase, so the result is treated as nearly settled. Second, 60 off 30 with 3 wickets gets WP 0.066 and low leverage (0.014): a desperate chase is dramatic but not high-leverage, because the game is nearly lost already. The first-innings 25/3 state gets WP 0.274, lower than a simple par projection would suggest; the model penalises three early wickets heavily. These are model outputs, not hand-set values, and they are why Stage 4 uses state-adjusted expectations and shrinkage rather than raw situational intuition.

The 18-off-6 state is labelled **bowler_pressure** even though it is a tense chase, because the batting side's pre-ball WP is 0.58 (above 0.5). That is the definition the review requested; the side label is descriptive, not a claim about who will win the ball.

## 9. Limitations

- LI inherits the win-probability model's error, especially in the innings-1 powerplay where AUC is 0.60.
- Outcome probabilities are league-average by state, not by batter, bowler, venue or team strength. That is deliberate: LI must not depend on identity.
- DLS/shortened and incompletely recorded matches are excluded; tied and super-over matches are reserved for the Stage 7 sensitivity run.
- The 100-ball cell minimum is a bias-variance choice; the pooled and within-innings normalisations are both reported.
